The Science of In-Place Tooling

What is Outside Machining?

In traditional manufacturing, workpieces travel to static machine shops. However, marine propulsion shafts weighing 20 tons, stern tubes welded into a ship's hull, or massive deck winches cannot simply be unbolted and trucked away without weeks of structural cutting and exorbitant crane mobilization.

Outside Machining (also known as field machining or in-situ machining) is the engineering specialty of rigidly mounting high-precision portable machine tools directly onto the vessel's hull or structural members.

Brothers Machine Services Corp deploys specialized boring bars, facing heads, journal lathes, and optical alignment scopes that replicate the accuracy of multi-million dollar shop milling centers right inside the drydock or ship's hold.

Propulsion & Rotating Systems

On-Site Shaft & Journal Turning

Bearing failure or foreign object entanglement often scores propulsion shafts and journals. Removing a shaft requires pulling the propeller, rudder, and couplings—costing weeks in dock. Our orbital turning machines wrap around the shaft to cut and polish journals in place.

  • • Orbital Journal Turning: Lathe-quality finish machined without rotating the propulsion shaft.
  • • Propeller Shaft Taper Fitting: Dialing and bluing propeller hub tapers for 85%+ blue contact fit.
  • • Intermediate & Generator Shafts: Restoring bearing seat diameters, seal areas, and thrust collars.
  • • On-Site Keyway Milling: Milling damaged or new propeller shaft keyways in the drydock.
